Affidavits for Marriage Registration: What They Declare and Why Registrars Ask for Them

Most marriage registration offices in India ask the couple for an affidavit, sometimes one each and sometimes a joint one. It is a sworn statement of facts that the registrar cannot easily check from documents alone, and false statements in it can carry consequences.

General information, not legal advice. Exact formats differ by state, by law and by office.

What an affidavit usually declares

For a marriage under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 or the Special Marriage Act, 1954, affidavits commonly state:

  • full name, address and date of birth of the deponent, matching the identity and age documents;
  • marital status at the time of marriage: unmarried, divorced or widowed;
  • that neither party has a living spouse;
  • that the parties are not within the degrees of prohibited relationship and, for Hindu marriages, not sapindas of each other, unless a custom governing both permits it;
  • that each party is capable of giving valid consent;
  • nationality and, where relevant, the date and place of the marriage already solemnised.

Supporting documents that go with it

  • Divorced: a copy of the final divorce decree.
  • Widowed: the death certificate of the former spouse.
  • Foreign national or NRI: passport and visa details, and sometimes a certificate of marital status from the embassy or local authority.

Who signs and where

The affidavit is sworn before a notary or an authorised officer, on stamp paper of the value fixed by state law. Each party signs their own, or both sign a joint one where the office accepts that format.

Why accuracy matters

The registrar relies on these statements. A false affidavit can lead to refusal of registration, cancellation later, and liability for making a false statement on oath. Small errors, such as a misspelt name or a wrong date, also cause delays because the affidavit must match every other document.

Practical points

  1. Ask the specific registrar's office for its current format before getting the affidavit prepared.
  2. Use exactly the same spelling of names and dates as on the age and identity documents.
  3. Keep a copy of every affidavit filed; it may be needed later for a passport, visa or name change.
